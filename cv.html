About me

Mathematics, statistics
and public health.

I am a quantitative researcher working at the intersection of medical statistics, mathematical modelling and epidemiology. My work combines mathematical and statistical methods to investigate complex health processes, analyse clinical and population-level data, and support evidence-informed public health decisions.

Research profile

From mathematical theory to health applications

My background is in mathematics, with a PhD focused on mathematical modelling of epidemics. This work involved theoretical analysis, network models and applications to real epidemiological data, including systems evolving on multiple time scales.

Over time, my research has expanded across complementary areas: epidemic dynamics on networks, the spread of information and misinformation, medical statistics and clinical epidemiology, health technology assessment, and mechanistic modelling in biomedical applications. I am particularly interested in combining rigorous quantitative methods with questions that matter for healthcare and public health.
