About me

Mathematics, statistics
and public health.

I am a quantitative researcher working at the intersection of medical statistics, mathematical modelling and epidemiology. My work combines mathematical and statistical methods to investigate complex health processes, analyse clinical and population-level data, and support evidence-informed public health decisions.

Research profile

From mathematical theory to health applications

My background is in mathematics, with a PhD focused on mathematical modelling of epidemics. This work involved theoretical analysis, network models and applications to real epidemiological data, including systems evolving on multiple time scales.

Over time, my research has expanded across complementary areas: epidemic dynamics on networks, the spread of information and misinformation, medical statistics and clinical epidemiology, health technology assessment, and mechanistic modelling in biomedical applications. I am particularly interested in combining rigorous quantitative methods with questions that matter for healthcare and public health.

Selected projects

Research in practice

My work combines mathematical modelling, medical statistics and epidemiology. These selected projects illustrate how quantitative methods can address questions ranging from disease dynamics and clinical research to prevention strategies and public health decisions.

01 · Mathematical Modelling of Epidemics

Analysis of epidemic models based on ordinary differential equations (ODEs), including multi-group systems, network and graph-based models, stability analysis and dynamics evolving on multiple time scales. My work also uses Geometric Singular Perturbation Theory (GSPT) to investigate the behaviour of complex dynamical systems.

02 · International Cohort Studies: ACC, INHANCE and IESC3

Coordination, data management, quality control, data harmonization and statistical analysis of international pooled datasets. Within the Asia Cohort Consortium (ACC), this included work on coffee and tea consumption and pancreatic cancer mortality.

03 · Cardiovascular Prevention — FItPreV

Statistical and epidemiological analysis of a randomised controlled pilot trial evaluating personalised cardiovascular prevention strategies and their potential implementation within the Italian National Health Service. Collaboration: Università Cattolica del Sacro Cuore.

04 · Vaccine Evaluation and Health Technology Assessment

Health Technology Assessment and cost-effectiveness analyses of influenza vaccination strategies, including economic evaluations for the Italian and Spanish contexts. Collaborations included VIHTALI, Evidera, CSL Seqirus and academic and public-health partners involved in vaccine evaluation.

05 · ORCHESTRA: COVID-19 Vaccine Response

Statistical analysis of immunological response and antibody kinetics following SARS-CoV-2 vaccination in healthcare workers, within the ORCHESTRA project.

06 · Cervical Cancer Biopsy Decision Support

Research collaboration with the University of Bologna and AIRC, developing an analytical framework combining principal component analysis (PCA), patient clustering and random-forest models to support risk-stratified biopsy decision-making.

07 · Therapeutic Antibody Immunogenicity — COST Action ENOTTA

Mechanistic mathematical modelling of therapeutic antibodies in chronic inflammatory diseases, including the effect of methotrexate on adalimumab immunogenicity in axial spondyloarthritis. This work is connected to the European COST Action ENOTTA (European Network on Optimising Treatment with Therapeutic Antibodies in Chronic Inflammatory Diseases).

08 · Avian Influenza Transmission Networks

Mathematical modelling of avian influenza transmission networks across poultry farms in Veneto and Lombardy. Collaboration: University of Trento and Istituto Zooprofilattico Sperimentale delle Venezie (IZSVe).

09 · Wolbachia-Based Control of Aedes albopictus

Analysis of field data from releases of Wolbachia-infected male mosquitoes to assess fertility reduction in wild populations in Italy. Collaboration: Sapienza University of Rome, ENEA, University of Trento and FBK.

10 · BRIC-2022-ID25: Return to Work After Liver Transplantation

Statistical analysis of a patient dataset combining clinical data and interview data, within an interdisciplinary project investigating risk factors for unemployment and return to work among liver transplant recipients.
